# Reconstruction Plan Report

## Executive Summary
The earthquake caused widespread destruction, particularly in Hama Street, Central Nishikigawa Street, and South Central Asaichi Street, where buildings sustained severe structural damage and debris obstructed roads. The concrete bridge collapsed completely, posing significant risks. Immediate response efforts focused on clearing debris, stabilizing collapsed structures, and assessing safety to facilitate emergency access. The Wajima Drama Memorial Hall remained undamaged, serving as a temporary shelter for displaced residents. Reconstruction plans will prioritize debris removal, structural repairs, and road rehabilitation to restore functionality and safety.

## Assessment of Damage
The earthquake caused widespread destruction, primarily affecting Hama Street, Central Nishikigawa Street, and South Central Asaichi Street. Buildings in these locations sustained severe structural damage, with debris obstructing roads and causing significant disruptions. The concrete bridge collapsed completely, posing severe risks to passersby. The Wajima Drama Memorial Hall remained undamaged, serving as a temporary shelter for displaced residents. The disaster report indicates that the Wajima Drama Memorial Hall, Hama Street, Central Nishikigawa Street, and South Central Asaichi Street were classified as G4 and G5 based on their structural damage and debris obstruction. The affected areas require immediate attention to debris clearance, road repairs, and safety assessments to restore functionality and safety. ## Reconstruction Needs
The reconstruction needs for the affected areas include debris removal, structural repairs, and road rehabilitation. For Hama Street, Central Nishikigawa Street, and South Central Asaichi Street, which were classified as G4 and G5, structural repairs will focus on stabilizing partially collapsed buildings and removing debris to restore road access. The Wajima Drama Memorial Hall, classified as G1, requires no structural repairs. For the collapsed concrete bridge, immediate intervention is needed to prevent further hazards and ensure safe access. Local authorities should prioritize debris removal and road repairs to facilitate emergency response and recovery efforts. [SPHERE-3-p1], [SPHERE-5-p1], [SPHERE-5-p2]

## Plan of Action
The Plan of Action outlines the detailed reconstruction steps, prioritizing debris removal, structural repairs, and road rehabilitation to restore functionality and safety in the affected areas. First, we will mobilize specialized teams to clear debris from Hama Street, Central Nishikigawa Street, and South Central Asaichi Street, ensuring safe access for emergency responders and preventing further hazards. Concurrently, structural engineers will assess and stabilize partially collapsed buildings in these locations, focusing on critical safety measures to prevent collapses. For the collapsed concrete bridge, immediate intervention is required to remove debris and secure the area, enabling the deployment of temporary bridges for emergency access. Local authorities will be involved in coordinating these efforts, ensuring compliance with building codes and safety standards. Subsequently, we will initiate road rehabilitation projects, addressing debris blockages and reconstructing damaged sections to restore connectivity. Throughout the process, community engagement will be crucial to ensure informed decision-making and successful recovery. [SPHERE-3-p1], [SPHERE-5-p1], [SPHERE-5-p2]

## Recommendations
To ensure effective and efficient reconstruction, it is recommended to establish a dedicated task force comprising local authorities, structural engineers, and disaster management experts to oversee the debris removal, structural repairs, and road rehabilitation efforts. Funding should be allocated for specialized equipment and materials to expedite debris clearance and support structural stabilization. Additionally, community engagement programs should be implemented to gather input on rebuilding preferences and ensure transparency in reconstruction decisions. Long-term investments in resilient infrastructure and improved building codes will mitigate future risks and enhance disaster preparedness.
