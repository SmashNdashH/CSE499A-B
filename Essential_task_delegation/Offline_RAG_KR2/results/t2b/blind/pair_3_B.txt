In terms of repair, retrofit, or rebuild, the damage assessment suggests that the majority of the structures have suffered Grade 5 destruction, necessitating a comprehensive approach. According to the Sphere Handbook 2018 (SPHERE-appendix-4-p1), a range of context-specific assistance options can be combined to meet the needs of affected people. For Grade 5 destruction, repair alone may not suffice, and retrofitting might be necessary to strengthen and modify the buildings' structure to make them more resistant to future hazards. The Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-5-p2) emphasizes the importance of considering the need for specialized professional expertise, adhering to building codes and standards, and increasing technical capacity among the affected population. For Grade 2 to 4 damage, the Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-5-p1) recommends promoting safer building practices to meet current shelter needs and reduce future risks. The EMS-98 guidelines (EMS98-2.3-p2) highlight that a building's vulnerability increases with irregularity and asymmetry, which can contribute to damage during earthquakes. Therefore, retrofitting may be necessary to address these vulnerabilities and enhance the resilience of the structures.

In conclusion, the Santa Rosa wildfire has resulted in severe damage requiring immediate attention. The hot spot, Safe Areas 1, 2, and 3, and potentially other areas with Grade 2 to 4 damage necessitate a coordinated response involving rescue operations, reconstruction, and technical assistance

## Reconstruction Needs
To address the severe damage caused by the Santa Rosa wildfire, a comprehensive reconstruction plan is essential. The Sphere Handbook 2018 (SPHERE-appendix-4-p1) outlines various assistance options, including repair, shelter kits, and retrofitting, depending on the extent of the damage. Given the high percentage of Grade 5 destruction (67%) and Grade 2 to 4 damage (2%), a multi-faceted approach is necessary.

**Repair and Retrofit:**
For the Grade 5 destruction, repair alone may not be sufficient. Retrofitting is recommended to strengthen and modify the structures to enhance their resilience against future hazards. This includes assessing the structural integrity and addressing any weaknesses identified in the hot spot and other severely damaged areas. Local authorities and technical experts should be involved in the retrofitting process to ensure compliance with building codes and standards. The Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-5-p1) emphasizes the importance of promoting safer building practices and engaging the affected population in the rebuilding process.

**Shelter Kits and Supplies:**
For the Grade 2 to 4 damage, shelter kits containing construction material, tools, and fixtures are necessary. These kits should be designed to improve living spaces while ensuring safety and durability. The Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-5-p2) highlights the need for technical assistance to ensure that the shelters meet safe building practices. Local materials and labor should be utilized to minimize costs and maximize community involvement.

**Emergency Shelters:**
Safe Areas 1, 2, and 3, while offering some relief, still require thorough inspection. Emergency shelters should be established in these areas to provide temporary housing for displaced individuals. The Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-3-p1) provides guidance on the minimum living space requirements, which should be adhered to. Local authorities should be involved in the planning and implementation of these shelters to ensure they meet the needs of the affected population.

**Medical Facilities:**
Medical points and hospitals should be set up in Safe Areas 1 and 2 to provide immediate medical care and triage for casualties. The Sphere Handbook (SPHERE-3-p1) recommends establishing triage points and ensuring that medical supplies are readily available. Local medical personnel should be consulted to develop a comprehensive medical evacuation plan, including the repatriation of rescued individuals to their home countries for further treatment.

**Transportation Recovery:**
The most critical road connecting Safe Area 1 to the rest of the city must be reopened to facilitate the flow of resources and personnel. The Emergency Service Team should coordinate with local authorities to ensure that this road is cleared and safe for vehicular traffic. Additionally, the road leading to Safe Area 3 should be opened to ensure efficient access to the most severely damaged areas.

In summary, the reconstruction of the Santa Rosa wildfire area requires a coordinated effort involving repair and retrofitting, provision of shelter kits and supplies, establishment of emergency shelters, and the setup of medical facilities. Technical assistance and community involvement are crucial to ensure that the rebuilt structures are safe and resilient. While specific staff, team, shelter, and supply numbers are not available offline, the Sphere Handbook and EMS-98 guidelines provide a framework for a comprehensive and effective reconstruction plan.

## Recommendations
To support an effective and efficient reconstruction, it is crucial to allocate resources strategically and engage the community in the rebuilding process. Policy-wise, establishing clear guidelines and standards for reconstruction, including adherence to building codes and standards, will ensure that structures are safe and resilient. Funding should prioritize immediate needs such as emergency shelters, medical facilities, and transportation recovery. Long-term support should focus on repair, retrofitting, and rebuilding, with a focus on sustainable materials and community engagement. Community-led initiatives should be encouraged to promote local ownership and participation in the rebuilding process. Adequate funding should also be allocated for environmental sustainability measures and livelihood opportunities to support the affected population.
