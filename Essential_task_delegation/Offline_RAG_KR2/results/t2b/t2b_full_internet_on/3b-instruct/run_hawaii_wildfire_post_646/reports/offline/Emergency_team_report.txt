#### Shelter Planning
According to the Sphere Handbook 2018, the living space standard for emergency shelters is 3.5 square meters per person, excluding cooking space, bathing area, and sanitation facility. Given the relatively intact buildings, we can assume that these areas can accommodate a certain number of people safely. For the hotspots, immediate attention is needed to secure and stabilize the structures. For the safe areas, the living space should be designed to ensure privacy and dignity, with appropriate access to basic services and livelihoods opportunities.

#### Site Selection
For the safe areas, the site selection should adhere to the guidelines provided by [SPHERE-2-p4] (Sphere Handbook 2018, Shelter and Settlement, pp. 284-288). The minimum usable surface area for each person in camp-type settlements is 45 square meters, including household plots, roads, footpaths, external cooking areas, communal cooking areas, education and recreation areas, healthcare facilities, sanitation, firebreaks, administration, water storage, site drainage, religious facilities, food distribution areas, markets, storage, and limited kitchen gardens for individual households (excluding significant agricultural activities or livestock). The minimum usable surface area for each person in urban settings is 30 square meters, including household plots, roads, footpaths, and essential services.

The shelter construction should be lightweight with a low thermal capacity, such as timber, and use raised floors to prevent water entering the covered living area. The internal floor-to-ceiling height should be at least 2 meters (2.6 meters in hot climates) at the highest point. The shelter should have appropriate drainage facilities to keep dwelling areas free of standing water and stormwater drains clear.
