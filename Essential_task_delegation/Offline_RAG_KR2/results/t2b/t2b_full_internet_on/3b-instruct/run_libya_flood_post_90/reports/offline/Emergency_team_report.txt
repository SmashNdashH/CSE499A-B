# Emergency Service Team Report: Shelters, Hospitals and Transportation Recovery

## Emergency shelters
Based on the satellite imagery and the hot spots identified, the northern part of the photo offers three safe areas where emergency shelters can be established. These areas are critical for ensuring the safety and well-being of the affected population. Safe Area 1, located around (493, 273), is approximately 420 pixels from the nearest estimated damage. Safe Area 2, situated around (466, 100), is just 398 pixels from the nearest damage. Safe Area 3, positioned around (627, 341), is 353 pixels from the nearest damage. These areas are considered relatively unaffected by the flood but require thorough inspection to confirm their safety. They offer ample space for emergency shelters, adhering to the Sphere guidelines on living space and site area per person.

The northern part of the photo also contains buildings that are estimated to have suffered negligible to slight damage (EMS-98 Grade 1). These buildings are distributed throughout the photo but are not concentrated in any specific hot spot. The distribution of the estimated damage suggests that the flood caused widespread destruction, particularly in the southern regions, while the northern part of the photo remained relatively unscathed. Given this, the northern part of the photo presents ideal conditions for establishing emergency shelters. The buildings in this area can be assessed for their suitability and potential to accommodate emergency shelters, ensuring that each affected household has adequate living space to perform basic domestic activities, such as sleeping, food preparation, and eating, while maintaining privacy and dignity.

According to the Sphere Handbook 2018, the minimum living space should reflect cultural and social norms, the context, the phase of response, and guidance by national authorities or the humanitarian response sector. Not available offline, but generally, the minimum living space should be 3.5 square meters per person, excluding cooking space, bathing area, and sanitation facility. In cold climates or urban settings where internal cooking space and bathing and/or sanitation facilities are included, the minimum living space should be 4.5 to 5.5 square meters per person. The internal floor-to-ceiling height should be at least 2 meters (2.6 meters in hot climates) at the highest point. The northern part of the photo, with its relatively undamaged buildings, provides sufficient space for emergency shelters, meeting these minimum standards.

The location and access of these shelters should be carefully planned to ensure that they are easily accessible and provide safe access to fundamental activities. Key actions include ensuring that the space immediately surrounding the living space supports safe access to cooking, toilets, laundry, bathing, livelihoods activities, socialising, and play areas. The shelters should also be designed to accommodate the diverse needs of household members, promoting the use of shelter solutions, construction techniques, and materials that are culturally and socially acceptable and environmentally sustainable. ## Immediate transportation recovery
Based on the satellite imagery and the hot spots identified, the most critical areas for immediate transportation recovery are the southern regions, specifically Hot Spots 1, 2, and 3. These areas are severely damaged and pose significant risks to road and bridge safety. Hot Spot 1, around (902, 685), and Hot Spot 2, around (565, 766), contain the highest concentrations of destroyed buildings, indicating extensive damage to infrastructure. Hot Spot 3, around (682, 991), also shows substantial destruction. Reopening roads and bridges in these areas will be crucial for facilitating emergency response efforts, evacuations, and delivering aid supplies. Additionally, the northern part of the photo, while relatively safe, still requires assessment to ensure it meets the Sphere guidelines for living space and site area per person. Prioritizing transportation recovery in the southern regions will help minimize risks and improve overall response efficiency.
