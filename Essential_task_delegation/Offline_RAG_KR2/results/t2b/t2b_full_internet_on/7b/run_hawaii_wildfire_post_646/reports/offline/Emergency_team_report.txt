# Emergency Service Team Report: Shelters, Hospitals and Transportation Recovery

## Emergency shelters
The emergency shelters should be established in the safe areas identified in the north-east of the photo, specifically in the vicinity of safe area 1, safe area 2, and safe area 3. These zones are 280, 218, and 147 pixels respectively from the nearest estimated damage, ensuring that they remain unaffected by the wildfire. According to Sphere Handbook 2018, Shelter and Settlement, the minimum living space should be 3.5 square metres per person, excluding cooking space, bathing area, and sanitation facilities. This will enable essential household and livelihood activities to be performed with dignity. Furthermore, the area should provide access to fundamental activities such as cooking, toilets, laundry, bathing, and socializing, as outlined in Sphere Handbook 2018, Shelter and Settlement, pp. 289-292. The safe areas identified are suitable for establishing shelters due to their proximity to intact infrastructure and their lack of visible damage from the wildfire.

## Hospitals
Based on the guidelines from INSARAG, it is essential to establish medical facilities in safe areas to handle the rescued people effectively. Given the extent of the disaster, it is recommended to prioritize the north region, where safe areas 1, 2, and 3 are located, for setting up temporary medical points. These areas are far from the hotspots and have not been significantly affected by the wildfire, ensuring the safety of medical staff and patients. According to INSARAG [INSARAG3-6.2-p2], it is crucial to check medical evacuation plans, conduct risk assessments, and maintain hygiene standards at these medical points. Additionally, medical staff must be aware of local procedures for accepting patients and coordinating with local hospitals for further treatment. The medical teams should also be prepared to handle potential injuries and illnesses resulting from the disaster, ensuring that patients receive timely and effective care.

## Immediate transportation recovery
The immediate transportation recovery should prioritize reopening the road network in the north region, specifically in the vicinity of safe areas 1, 2, and 3. These areas are located away from the hotspots and have not been significantly affected by the wildfire, ensuring the safety of emergency responders and facilitating the movement of supplies and personnel. Reopening these roads will enable efficient access to the newly established emergency shelters and medical facilities, allowing for the coordination of relief efforts and the transport of affected individuals for medical care.
