# Emergency Service Team Report: Shelters, Hospitals and Transportation Recovery

## Emergency shelters
The emergency shelters should be established in the safe areas identified in the north-west and west of the photo, as these zones are farthest from the estimated damage and provide ample space for displaced residents. According to Sphere Handbook 2018, Shelter and Settlement [SPHERE-3-p1] and [SPHERE-3-p2], each affected household requires a minimum of 3.5 square metres of living space to perform basic domestic activities, with an internal floor-to-ceiling height of at least 2 metres. The safe areas in question meet these criteria, ensuring that displaced individuals have access to adequate living spaces that are safe and dignified. The proximity of these safe zones to the affected area facilitates swift access for relief teams and supplies, aligning with Sphere Handbook 2018, Shelter and Settlement [SPHERE-2-p1] and [SPHERE-2-p2] guidelines on location and settlement planning.

## Hospitals
Based on the report of the Expert team and the guidelines from INSARAG, it is crucial to establish temporary medical facilities in safe areas to handle rescued individuals. Given the absence of intact buildings, no hospitals are currently functional, and thus, temporary medical points must be set up in the safe zones identified in the north-west and west of the photo to provide immediate care. These locations are farthest from the estimated damage and offer ample space for treating displaced residents. According to INSARAG [INSARAG3-6.2-p2], it is essential to check medical evacuation plans, conduct risk assessments, and ensure hygiene standards are maintained at these temporary medical points. Additionally, medical staff must be aware of local procedures for accepting patients into the local system and develop a system for repatriation to the home country for further treatment.

## Immediate transportation recovery
The immediate transportation recovery should prioritize reopening the road network in the north-west and west of the photo, as these areas are identified as safe zones with ample space for displaced residents. Given the proximity of these zones to the affected area, reopening these roads will facilitate swift access for relief teams and supplies, ensuring efficient delivery of aid and support to the impacted population. Additionally, the safe areas in these regions meet the criteria for establishing emergency shelters and medical facilities, further justifying the priority of road reopening in these zones.
