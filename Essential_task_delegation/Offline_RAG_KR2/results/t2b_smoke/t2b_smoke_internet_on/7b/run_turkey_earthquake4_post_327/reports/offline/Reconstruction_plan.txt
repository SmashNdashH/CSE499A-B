## Assessment of Damage
The earthquake caused significant damage, with 33% of buildings completely destroyed, 11% moderately damaged, and 56% remaining intact. The most severe impact was observed in the central region, where the highest density of damage occurred, as evidenced by three distinct hotspots: one in the north-west, another in the west, and the third in the north-east of the image. These hotspots collectively cover 403997, 22107, and 28381 pixels, respectively, indicating the most concentrated areas of destruction. The majority of destroyed structures exhibited total collapse, while damaged buildings displayed moderate to heavy structural and non-structural damage, including partial collapses and extensive wall cracks. Safe areas in the south-east are being utilized for emergency shelters. Based on the disaster grades provided by the Expert team, buildings estimated destroyed are classified as [EMS98-masonry-grade-5], while buildings estimated damaged are classified as [EMS98-masonry-grade-2], [EMS98-masonry-grade-3], and [EMS98-masonry-grade-4]. The intact buildings show no visible damage, at most [EMS98-masonry-grade-1]. These findings align with the guidelines on disaster grading [EMS98-masonry-grade-5, EMS98-masonry-grade-2, EMS98-masonry-grade-3, EMS98-masonry-grade-4, EMS98-masonry-grade-1].

## Reconstruction Needs
The reconstruction plan requires immediate attention to the hotspots in the north-west, west, and north-east of the image, where the majority of structural collapse and debris are concentrated. For these areas, the primary focus should be on debris clearance and stabilization of damaged buildings to prevent further risks. Based on the Sphere Handbook 2018 guidelines [SPHERE-5-p1], technical assistance is essential to guide the affected population in repairing and retrofitting structures to mitigate future risks. Efforts should also include monitoring intact buildings for hidden vulnerabilities to ensure overall safety. In the south-east, where safe areas have been identified, emergency shelters should be established to support displaced residents, aligning with Sphere Handbook 2018 recommendations [SPHERE-1-p4]. Local authorities and community involvement are key to ensuring reconstruction efforts are effective and sustainable.

## Plan of Action
### Immediate Actions
1. **Debris Clearance:** Mobilize emergency response teams to clear debris from the hotspots in the north-west, west, and north-east of the image. Prioritize safe access routes to ensure efficient removal of rubble and prevent secondary hazards. Utilize specialized equipment and local labor to expedite the process.
2. **Stabilization of Damaged Buildings:** Deploy structural engineers to assess and stabilize partially collapsed buildings in the hotspots. Implement temporary reinforcement measures to prevent further collapse and ensure safety for rescue operations. Focus on buildings classified as [EMS98-masonry-grade-2] to [EMS98-masonry-grade-4].
3. **Emergency Shelter Setup:** Establish temporary shelters in safe areas 1, 2, and 3 in the south-east of the image. Provide basic amenities and ensure hygiene and sanitation. Coordinate with local authorities to distribute relief supplies and monitor the well-being of displaced residents.

### Short-term Actions
4. **Structural Repairs:** Engage local contractors and skilled laborers to begin repairs on damaged buildings, focusing on buildings classified as [EMS98-masonry-grade-2] to [EMS98-masonry-grade-4]. Develop a phased plan to address structural integrity, using materials and techniques compliant with local building codes.
5. **Community Training:** Organize training sessions for affected residents on basic earthquake-resistant building practices and safety protocols. Empower them to participate in the reconstruction process through hands-on workshops and practical demonstrations.
6. **Monitoring Intact Structures:** Conduct periodic inspections of intact buildings to identify potential hidden vulnerabilities. Develop a monitoring program to track structural stability and implement necessary interventions to mitigate risks. Collaborate with local authorities to establish a sustainable framework for ongoing safety checks.

### Long-term Actions
7. **Building Retrofitting:** For severely damaged buildings, initiate retrofitting projects to enhance structural resilience against future earthquakes. Utilize advanced materials and techniques as per [SPHERE-5-p1] to strengthen foundations and reinforce load-bearing components. Foster collaboration between local authorities and experts to ensure compliance with updated building codes.
8. **Capacity Building:** Invest in capacity-building programs for local builders and engineers to adopt modern earthquake-resistant designs. Promote the development of regional standards and guidelines to ensure consistent application of safe construction practices. Encourage community participation in planning and execution of reconstruction projects.
9. **Review and Adaptation:** Regularly review progress and adapt reconstruction strategies based on feedback from affected communities and expert assessments. Establish a transparent communication channel to address concerns and ensure alignment with long-term goals. Foster a culture of resilience by integrating disaster preparedness measures into routine urban planning.

## Recommendations
To ensure effective and efficient reconstruction, we recommend allocating funds for immediate debris clearance and stabilization efforts in the hotspots, supported by specialized teams and equipment. Long-term investments should focus on retrofitting severely damaged buildings to enhance earthquake resilience, guided by local building codes and expert consultation. Community engagement through training and capacity-building programs is essential for sustainable reconstruction. Additionally, establishing a robust monitoring system for intact structures will help identify and mitigate hidden vulnerabilities, ensuring the safety of both repaired and newly built homes.
